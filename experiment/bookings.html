What it is

A bookings service designed through a structured interview

A BookingService keyed by booking_id. The rules came from a three-round /rv interview that surfaced, among other things, the terminal-vs-nightmare conflict (a cancelled booking must stay watched so a later illegal check-in is caught).

Two rules are marked LOUD (paging): cancel-then-checkin and the 15-second promotion deadline. The rest are logged.

event model  · booking.status (eight lifecycle states) keyed by booking_id; the confirm event stamps balance_owed; the app's own capacity counter emits booking.cap_exceeded. No terminal - a 60s quiescence window stands in for end-of-day.

The sabotage

The bugs planted in the app code

Bug 1 - a one-letter typo in the confirm status

- self._status(booking_id, "confirmed", balance_owed=...)
+ self._status(booking_id, "comfirmed", balance_owed=...)

No confirmed event ever exists again. Three rules break at once: check-in after confirmed and attended after check-in false-alarm on every healthy booking (the prior confirm they require is missing), the promotion SLA pages because a confirm no longer counts as an answer, and never-confirm-while-owing goes dead - a real confirm-while-owing fault would now pass silently.

Policy in play: 02, 05 (loud) false-alarm; 04 dead  ·  Trigger: any reserve → confirm flow  ·  Class: normal-use

Bug 2 - capacity off-by-one

- if len(seats) > CLASS_CAPACITY:   # over 12
+ if len(seats) >= CLASS_CAPACITY:  # at 12

With CLASS_CAPACITY = 12, the 12th (legal, at-capacity) reservation now emits the cap-exceeded marker - a false over-capacity verdict one seat early.

Policy in play: 07 - class never over capacity  ·  Trigger: fill a class to exactly 12  ·  Class: subtle · edge

Detection

How the failure surfaced

The same defect through the framework's three independent channels, plus what the blind user actually noticed.

Channel A - live runtimemixed

Loud false alarms on healthy confirmed and promoted bookings (the promotion SLA paged), one rule silently dead, and the capacity alarm one seat early. The live board was actively misleading in both directions at once.

Channel B - replay gatered · exit 1

replay_check.py failed with two missing violations and four unexpected ones - the count moved decisively.

Channel C - stability contractrisk · exit 1

The stability strip read "risks", flagging the confirm and reserve emit sites as behavior-risk against the committed catalog.

The blind userfound 2 / 2

Demonstrated both live and via the gate: identified the typo as the root of the false alarms and the dead rule, and found the off-by-one by filling a class to exactly its cap.

A single typo in an emitted string can be more dangerous than a logic bug. It doesn't just hide a fault - it floods the operator with false pages on healthy traffic and silences a real rule, the fastest route to a monitor nobody trusts. The stability contract caught the drift; the false alarms were their own loud (if misleading) signal.
